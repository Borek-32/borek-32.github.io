ULTRASONIC AUDIO / REPORT-BACKED EVIDENCE

FPGA ultrasonic audio-array prototype

A Basys 3 and MATLAB system that sends 8-bit audio samples and a steering command to three hand-built H-bridge drivers feeding a 24-transducer, 40 kHz array. The retained demonstrator produced recognizable localized audio in an individual listening trial; controlled beam steering, calibrated acoustic performance, and PCB release remain open.

Executive summary

what was built

Problem

Explore localized ultrasonic audio with an FPGA-controlled array while keeping sample transport, steering commands, driver behavior, and release gates inspectable.

Implementation

MATLAB frames 64 unsigned 8-bit samples with a signed steering delta. FPGA logic drives three groups of eight 40 kHz transducers through hand-built full-bridge stages.

Result boundary

An individual listening trial reported recognizable audio concentrated in a preferred region. No controlled beam map was retained, and the redesigned driver remains blocked before fabrication.

System architecture

audio file to ultrasonic array

Serial control contract

At 115200 baud, each 68-byte frame carries A5, a signed 16-bit little-endian steering delta, 64 unsigned 8-bit samples, and 5A. This contract is documented independently of the missing rebuildable FPGA source.

Array geometry

The nominal 17.78-18 mm group pitch is more than four times the 4.29 mm half- wavelength at 40 kHz. Grating-lobe and angular ambiguity are therefore expected analytically even if the commanded electronic delay is correct.

  1. Prepare audio.MATLAB converts the source to mono, resamples, normalizes, and maps it to unsigned 8-bit samples.
  2. Calculate a command.The software converts the requested angle into a signed FPGA tick delta.
  3. Frame and transmit.Start and end markers bound each steering value and 64-sample payload.
  4. Decode on FPGA.The receiver separates steering and sample fields for the phase-control and modulation paths.
  5. Switch three phases.Three external full bridges drive eight transducer branches each.
  6. Treat steering as unverified.The command path exists, but the array spacing and missing controlled beam map prevent a steering-performance claim.

Prototype listening result

qualitative individual trial

The original demonstrator produced recognizable audio that the builder perceived as concentrated in a preferred region. This is useful prototype evidence, but it was not recorded with a calibrated microphone, controlled room geometry, blinded listeners, or synchronized electrical and acoustic captures.

How to read the listening result: it is a subjective observation from one demonstrator, not a calibrated directivity, SPL, intelligibility, beamwidth, or electronic steering measurement. No quantitative acoustic claim is made.

Known limitations and next proof

honest status

Known limitations

  • The listening result is qualitative and has no calibrated acoustic capture or controlled beam map.
  • The approximately 18 mm pitch is spatially undersampled for unique 40 kHz electronic steering.
  • Only eight Phase-A static capacitances are retained; the method, ESR/BVD, and Phase B/C values are missing.
  • The current nonlinear driver anchor fails its overlap/current gate, so zero fabrication hard passes exist.
  • The schematic is incomplete and no valid routed PCB, DRC closure, BOM, or manufacturing package exists.
  • The historical FPGA record is not currently rebuildable or timing-closed from retained source.

Best next evidence

  • Run the full 400/600 ns nonlinear deadtime, split-gate, feedback, and zero-state matrix.
  • Measure all 24 transducers with a recorded method, including ESR and complex impedance near 40 kHz.
  • Capture current-limited gate, switch-node, differential-output, source-current, and temperature waveforms.
  • Choose the driver topology, then close the one-phase schematic and ERC before routing a clean board.
  • Retain a calibrated microphone sweep or angular map with fixed geometry and synchronized electrical evidence.
  • Recover or reconstruct the FPGA project and publish a timing-closed build plus connector-level capture.
