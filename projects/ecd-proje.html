FPGA 3-bit ADC and VGA oscilloscope

A Basys3 design that closes the loop around a discrete analog front end: FPGA logic freezes a sample, selects three comparison references, reconstructs a 3-bit code, finds a repeatable waveform boundary, and draws the captured result on VGA. The record below separates the current routed build from historical bench photographs.

Executive summary

what was built

Problem

Demonstrate an end-to-end ADC in which discrete analog acquisition, FPGA decision sequencing, trigger alignment, and a live display operate as one explainable signal path.

Implementation

A sample-and-hold and comparator feed a three-step controller. Completed codes drive a low-to-high trigger detector, alternating 256-sample buffers, and a staircase VGA renderer.

Result boundary

Historical photographs show four waveform families on VGA, while the current package-inverting source set produces a routed bitstream. The current build is not timing-closed or tied to those photographs.

Implemented architecture

analog input to VGA

Mixed-signal boundary

The FPGA supplies HOLD and three natural-binary reference selections, then samples one comparator decision on din. In the current top level, HOLD and all selectors are inverted at the package boundary for one-stage external 5 V translators.

Capture convention

One code is completed every 48 µs. A trigger FSM advances only on completed codes; the display engine captures two detected periods or 256 samples into the inactive buffer before swapping it onto the VGA plot.

  1. Acquire the input.A BS250 sample switch and 33 nF storage capacitor follow the moving analog waveform.
  2. Freeze one sample.The controller asserts logical HOLD for the duration of the three comparison decisions.
  3. Select references.The SAR-style states choose midpoint and conditional reference levels through the external MUX and ladder.
  4. Assemble one code.The controller samples din three times, writes the MSB-to-LSB decisions, and pulses code_valid.
  5. Find a stable boundary.Completed codes feed a low/high threshold sequence that emits period_valid after a full cycle.
  6. Capture without tearing.The inactive 256-sample buffer records the next span and becomes visible only after capture completes.
  7. Render the trace.A 25 MHz pixel clock drives a 640 × 480 frame with axes and a three-pixel-thick staircase waveform.

Acquisition and display evidence

historical bench record

The retained photographs pair an oscilloscope view of each applied waveform with the VGA staircase display. They demonstrate shape tracking across four waveform families; they do not provide a calibrated code-versus-voltage table or prove the current build hash.

How to read these photographs: they are qualitative historical end-to-end evidence. No retained capture ties them to the current package-inverting bitstream, and no calibrated ADC transition or amplitude-error table is available.

Discrete analog front end

prototype and interface

The analog side uses a MOSFET sample switch, storage capacitor, five-transistor OTA-like comparison stage, CMOS regeneration, a resistor-ladder reference concept, and MOSFET control translation. The Basys3 closes the decision loop and supplies VGA.

Known limitations and next proof

honest status

Known limitations

  • The routed design does not meet overall setup timing and contains unrecognized generated-clock and unconstrained paths.
  • The historical VGA photographs are not tied to the current bitstream hash.
  • The loaded comparator-return level at K17 is not retained for the current interface.
  • The historical copper drawing remains a do-not-fabricate artifact because its inferred connectivity and DRC are unresolved.

Best next evidence

  • Replace the fabric-generated dclk with a 100 MHz clock-enable path and close all relevant timing constraints.
  • Program the current bitstream, record its hash, and retain a synchronized input/comparator/code/VGA capture.
  • Measure all eight loaded reference voltages plus K17 high/low levels, edge rate, and comparator-return current.
  • Route and DRC a verified schematic before generating any fabrication artwork.
